An offline forensic histology AI, not a cloud service

Most AI tools for histology image recognition send images to a server — for forensic practice working with images of real autopsy specimens, that is a problem in its own right, independent of model accuracy. littlEye AI takes the opposite route: both training and evaluation run through Core ML directly on an iPhone, iPad or Mac, an image never leaves the device, and the app has no networking layer and no telemetry.

The H&E slide dataset spans 27 organs and tissues; slides are reviewed by colleagues with an audit trail, and the final diagnosis is settled by majority vote. Training always sets aside an independent test set and reports a confusion matrix with per-class sensitivity and precision — students and physicians alike see exactly where a model fails, not only where it succeeds.

Readiness indicator

A colour-coded rating of the dataset shows whether you have enough varied images per class (30 or more) before you start training.

Encrypted sharing

.le3AI models and .le3set datasets are AES-GCM encrypted and carry their own metrics and audit trail — shared between colleagues via AirDrop or email.

Atlas of 103 diagnoses

A knowledge base with microscopic descriptions, key features and suggested conclusions, drawn from cited literature.

Image quantification (3.2–3.3)

Area, count and size of colour-defined structures measured on the image, a desmin IHC calculator and tools for publication: image quality control, threshold sensitivity, ICC and Bland–Altman.

Learning from corrections

A misread slide is added back to the dataset in one tap, so the next training round learns from it.

From slide to trained model

Import and dataset

Microphotographs (JPEG/PNG/TIFF/HEIC) are sorted by organ and diagnosis; a stain and magnification log keeps the data consistent.

Colleague review

Multiple colleagues independently review slides; the app records the reviewer's name and date for each assessment (audit trail), and majority vote sets the final label.

On-device Core ML training

Configurable iterations and augmentations, an independent test set (10–20% stratified holdout), progress with the option to cancel.

Metrics and confusion matrix

Training, validation and test accuracy, plus sensitivity and precision reported separately for each class.

Export and sharing

The finished model is saved as an encrypted .le3AI file with a manifest — imported into another littlEye AI installation via Files, AirDrop or a double tap.

Built for case work as well as teaching

littlEye AI is not only a training tool — it also offers a full case workflow: scanner slides attached to a case number, case history and the post-mortem conclusion as inputs, a tissue check that flags a possible slide mix-up, and a concise summary of findings copied to the clipboard in one tap. An education module adds a "what next after H&E" guide, special stains, IHC markers, cellular elements and pigments — each entry carrying a source citation.

littlEye AI is developed in collaboration by three departments of the Faculty of Medicine, Comenius University in Bratislava. It is a research and decision-support tool for qualified professionals — not a medical device, and it does not provide a diagnosis; the final assessment always belongs to the pathologist or forensic physician.

Frequently asked questions

Does littlEye AI ship with ready-made diagnostic models?

No. The app is a training tool, not a model collection — it ships with a single demonstration model trained on synthetic patterns. Real models are trained from your own slides, or exchanged with colleagues as standalone encrypted .le3AI files.

Do slide microphotographs get sent to a server?

No. The entire app runs exclusively on-device — images, cases and trained models never leave it unless you explicitly export them. The app makes no network connections and has no telemetry or analytics.

How many images do I need to train a model?

The technical minimum is 2 classes with 5 images each, but the app recommends 30 or more images per class drawn from multiple cases — a colour-coded dataset-readiness indicator shows whether you have enough material before you start training.

Is littlEye AI a medical device?

No. It is a research and decision-support tool for qualified professionals. It does not provide a diagnosis — the final assessment always belongs to the pathologist or forensic physician.

Is littlEye AI free?

Yes, since version 3.0. No subscription, no feature behind a paywall — an optional one-time in-app contribution supports further development and unlocks nothing.
